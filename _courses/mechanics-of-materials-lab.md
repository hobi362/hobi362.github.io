The experimental mechanics lab — where the beam theory from lecture gets checked against
an instrumented specimen and does not always agree. Strain gage instrumentation, Wheatstone
bridge derivation and practice, LabVIEW data acquisition, and the cantilever shear and
moment work that makes the difference between a formula and a measurement obvious.

Five formal lab reports over the semester, each one a full write-up: procedure,
instrumentation, data reduction, and an honest account of where the numbers diverged from
prediction and why. This was the first course where I had to defend an uncertainty
estimate rather than just report a value.

It is the earliest thing on this site that clearly points forward. Error budgeting in
[2.77](/coursework/precision-machine-design/) — predicting how far off a machine will be
before you build it — is the same instinct, learned first on a strain gage and a beam.
