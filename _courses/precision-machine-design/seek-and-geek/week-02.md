This week I took a close look at an indoor rowing machine from Baker House — specifically
the flywheel/air-resistance mechanism and the rail system the seat rides on. The seat rail
has to handle repeated sliding loads smoothly for years without developing play, while the
flywheel and chain-and-pulley drivetrain need to translate a pulling force into consistent,
predictable resistance — the same kind of first-order mechanism thinking (contact,
constraint, and stiffness) that's at the center of this class, just applied to a machine
most people only think of as "cardio equipment."
