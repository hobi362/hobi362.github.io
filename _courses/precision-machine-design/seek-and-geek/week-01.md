I'd used my Zippo lighter for years and never actually taken it apart. George Blaisdell's
original design solved several real precision-engineering problems at once — a windproof
flame, a hinge that survives years of one-handed flipping without going sloppy, and a flint
wheel that has to deliver a repeatable, precisely-timed spark every time. Taking it apart
made obvious how much of the lighter's legendary reliability comes down to tight, deliberate
tolerancing on parts most people never look at twice.
