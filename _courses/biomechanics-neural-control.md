A quantitative, model-based look at how biomechanical and neural factors interact in human
sensory-motor behavior — comparing biological and robotic approaches to the same underlying
tasks. Coursework covered neural, muscular, and skeletal physiology, neural feedback and
equilibrium-point theories, co-contraction strategies, impedance control, kinematic
redundancy, optimization, intermittency, and contact/tool-use tasks.

My final project modeled realistic reaching movements in a robotic arm — directly relevant
to the impedance-control work I ended up doing later on RAWHIDE, where the same underlying
ideas about compliant, human-like arm behavior showed up again in a very different context.
