A summer at a product design consultancy in Delft, arranged through the **MIT MISTI**
Netherlands program. It was a different kind of engineering from the research I'd been
doing, much more about client work and fast concept iteration.

### How MMID designs

MMID is an integrated product design firm, headquartered in Delft with smaller offices in
Germany. Every product goes through three kinds of specialist designer working together:
look-and-feel designers make sure a person can use the product easily, functionality
designers make sure it actually works, and producibility designers make sure every part can
be made and assembled. I was on the functionality side.

### What I worked on

I was helping on projects from the first day, several at once and at different stages, from
early concepts to parts already in production:

- a **test rig for measuring eductor flow rates**: I assembled the design in SolidWorks and
  worked with suppliers to get the parts delivered on time
- concept development for a **high-pressure, tensile-proof pipe coupling**, where I did much
  of the sketching and brainstorming
- concept development for an **electrical outlet wall box**
- a **wall-mounted broom holder**
- **tensile testing of pipe clamps** already in production, confirming that the material
  choices held to spec

Hand sketching did a lot of the work. It was the quickest way to communicate an idea, and I
came home much better at it, and at the Adobe tools, than when I arrived. The other
adjustment was CAD: MMID has its own way of using SolidWorks to build clean, functional
models quickly, and learning it changed how I model.

### The work culture

What stayed with me most was the culture. The team was collaborative and flat, so I could
ask for more responsibility and move onto new projects without worrying about bureaucracy
or overstepping. Work-life balance is taken seriously in the Netherlands: many of my
colleagues worked fewer than 40 hours a week, some while also teaching at TU Delft, and the
team was no less productive for it. I left a more well-rounded designer, and I still use
parts of MMID's product innovation process in my own projects.
